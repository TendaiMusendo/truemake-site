The problem

Three sentences we hear every week

From founders, operations leads and engineering managers at companies with more process than people. Each one has a cost, and each one has a practical answer.

We bought the AI licences. Nobody uses them.

A chat window on its own has nothing to work with. It cannot see your customers, your calendar or your numbers, so people stop opening it.

What it costs: a subscription, and the hours it was meant to save.

The same customer is typed into three systems.

AI built on data that disagrees with itself produces confident nonsense. The plumbing has to be fixed before anything clever goes on top.

What it costs: wrong invoices, and nobody trusting the numbers.

Three months in and we still haven't seen it work.

Requirements, workshops, a build nobody can look at, then a big reveal that is wrong in ways you could have told them on day two. By go-live the team has stopped caring.

What it costs: the budget, and the adoption that was the whole point.

What "in daily use" looks like once it is running

A working day from our own business, where TrueMake's agents run against live records. The agent prepares. A person approves. Nothing leaves without a signature.

  1. 07:30Morning brief lands: today's meetings, what came in overnight, the three tasks that slipped yesterdayagent
  2. 09:05Client call ends. Notes become tasks, time entries and a project note in the tracker, as draftsagent
  3. 09:20Reads the drafts, fixes one estimate, approves the restperson
  4. 11:40Inbox triaged. Two replies drafted with the right customer record pulled in from the CRMagent
  5. 11:50Edits one reply, sends bothperson
  6. 16:45Day's hours booked to projects. Friday's invoice drafted from the booked timeagent
  7. 17:00Signs off the invoiceperson

Our standard

Four things we always do

Not a process diagram. These are the rules we run our own business by, and the reason an engagement ends with something in use rather than something in a slide.

1

Criteria before building

Every phase starts with a short document you sign: each outcome, how it is measured, and the evidence that closes it. "Done" is never an opinion.

2

Something to test early

You click through a working version on your own records well before the end, and keep doing so as it grows. If it cannot be shown, it is not progress.

3

Sandbox and a signature before production

Everything runs end to end on real records in a sandbox first. Agents draft and people approve, and that rule lives in the connector, not the prompt.

4

Handover you can run without us

Code, connectors and a runbook written for whoever maintains it next. Ongoing support is available, never required.
